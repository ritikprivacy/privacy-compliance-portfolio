Data Lifecycle & Flow Map — Collection to Deletion

Traces how personal data moves from the data subject, through internal departments and systems, to external recipients — identifying data category, controller/processor role, storage location and security controls at every phase. Built from the Data Discovery Questionnaire (FTPL-DPO-DDQ-2026-001) across 10 core business processes, structured on DPDP Act 2023 and ISO/IEC 27701 terminology.

Standards & terminology used in this map

Roles (DPDP Act 2023 · ISO/IEC 27701)

CONTROLLERData Fiduciary — FinSec, as the entity that determines the purpose and means of processing (DPDP Act S.2(9); ISO 27701 "PII Controller").
PROCESSORData Processor — a vendor engaged by FinSec to process data on its behalf and instructions only (DPDP Act S.2(17); ISO 27701 "PII Processor"), e.g. AWS, Twilio, HyperVerge.
JOINTJoint / Co-Controller — a party that independently determines some purpose of processing alongside FinSec, e.g. a Banking Partner holding the underlying account, or a Merchant for its own settlement data.
REGULATORExternal recipient under legal obligation — a regulator or authority receiving data by mandate, not as a processor, e.g. FIU-IND, RBI Ombudsman, UIDAI.

Data categories (risk-based tiering)

PIIOrdinary personal data identifying a data subject — name, email, mobile, address.
SENSITIVEIdentity, biometric or health-adjacent data warranting heightened controls — Aadhaar, PAN, biometric selfie, call recordings.
FINANCIALTransaction, balance or payment-instrument data, in PCI-DSS scope where card-adjacent.
NON-PIISystem-generated, aggregated or pseudonymised data with low re-identification risk.

Diagram notation (DFD standard)

 External entity — double-bordered box; a person or organisation outside FinSec's processing boundary (customer, beneficiary, merchant).
 Process — rounded box; a service, application or manual activity that transforms or routes data.
 Data store — open rectangle; a database or persistent store at rest.
 Trust boundary crossed — dashed border; data leaves FinSec's direct control to a processor, joint controller or regulator, sometimes across a border.
Note on terminology: the DPDP Act 2023 does not itself create a formal "sensitive personal data" sub-category (unlike the erstwhile SPDI Rules, 2011 or GDPR Art. 9). The PII / Sensitive / Financial / Non-PII tiering above is applied as an internal governance practice — aligned with ISO/IEC 27701 risk-based controls and PCI-DSS scoping — to calibrate proportionate safeguards, and is layered on top of, not a substitute for, DPDP Act obligations that apply uniformly to all personal data.

Diagram notation follows standard data-flow-diagram conventions (Gane–Sarson style) with trust-boundary framing consistent with common threat-modelling practice (e.g. Microsoft Threat Modeling Tool, OWASP Threat Dragon) — adapted here for privacy/data-flow mapping rather than security threat modelling.
